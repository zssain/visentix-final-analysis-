# Customer Journeys — What Using Visentix Actually Feels Like

Four walkthroughs, told from the customer's chair. Useful for onboarding (so builders feel the experience they're building), for the expert (so review work connects to real people), and for sales (as demo scripts).

---

## Journey 1 — Maria, Chief Privacy Officer at a mid-size retailer

*"I think our privacy notice is fine. I'd like to know, not think."*

**Day 1.** Maria's team submits their notice URL on the intake screen. A quiet "verified source" check appears; a progress stepper walks through Ingest → Decompose → Classify. On the right, her notice's clauses appear one by one, sorted by topic. She clicks one and sees the exact sentence highlighted in her document. First impression: *these people show their work.*

**Day 3.** Her draft is reviewed by the Visentix expert and approved. She opens the report. The cover shows an overall score, a confidence dial, and a snapshot ID. The executive summary tells her the honest story: solid on consumer rights, weak on retention and third-party sharing, 42nd percentile among 30 true peers.

**The moment that sells it.** In the Benchmark Language section, her sharing clause sits next to an anonymized clause from a stronger peer, differences highlighted: where she says "trusted partners," peers name the *categories* of recipients and *purposes*. She screenshots it for her general counsel. Nothing abstract — here's your sentence, here's a better sentence.

**The trust moment.** Her GC asks, "where does this 42nd percentile come from?" She clicks the number. A panel slides out: the clauses involved, the regulator concerns, the cohort (n=30, built June 19), the formula's plain-English description, the confidence rating. The GC nods. Meeting over.

**Ongoing.** Maria subscribes to monitoring. Two months later the feed shows: "Score moved 41 → 38" in teal (improving — her team shipped fixes) and a regulator-signal entry about an FTC action relevant to her weakest domain. She forwards the alert to legal before legal has even seen the news.

## Journey 2 — David, partner at a privacy consulting firm (white-label)

*"My clients ask for benchmarking. I don't have a data platform. I don't want to build one."*

David's firm signs up as a white-label partner. In the partner portal he creates a workspace for each client, uploads a client's notice, and generates a report carrying *his firm's* logo — same engine, same receipts, same expert-reviewed rigor underneath. The benchmark data feed also powers his firm's own quarterly client briefings. His pitch to clients: "we now bring market-wide privacy intelligence to every engagement." His margin: the remediation consulting the report's recommendations create.

## Journey 3 — Priya, policy analyst at a state regulator (future / bulk journey)

*"I want to know how an entire sector discloses tracking, not one company."*

Priya uploads a list of 200 companies in a sector. The system runs them all and returns a heat map: which disclosure domains are weakest across the sector, which companies are outliers, which gaps are most common. Every flag links to the actual clause language as evidence. She reads the Quarterly Intelligence Report each quarter for the industry-wide trends and cites its methodology page in her briefings — because it states its corpus size, its peer-group construction, and its confidence bands out loud.

## Journey 4 — Our own expert, on a review morning

*Included because the expert is a user too, and the workbench experience matters.*

Coffee, workbench open: "Queue: 4 findings pending." For each finding, three panes — the source clause on the left, the machine's analysis in the middle, the advisor note editor on the right. Two findings are clearly right: **Confirm**, twice. One overstates severity: **Edit**, soften the note, done. One is a false positive: **Dismiss** — it vanishes from the client's report. Each action quietly becomes training data; the counters at the top tick up.

One clause is a candidate exemplar, but the screen flags "john@doe.com [email]" with a lock icon — approval is blocked. One click replaces it with [REDACTED]; approval unlocks. The queue empties: "All findings reviewed." The expert approves the report; the gold DRAFT watermark disappears and the teal Reproducible mark takes its place. Total time: twenty minutes. The client gets a machine's thoroughness with a human's name-worthy judgment — which is the whole company, in one morning.

## What all four journeys share

The customer never has to *believe* us — every claim carries its evidence one click away. The honest bits (cohort sizes, confidence labels, "no changes since June") aren't fine print; they're the personality. And the expert's judgment is visible in the product, not a marketing claim.
